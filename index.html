§ 01

About

I'm an economics graduate working as a REDI Economic Development Analyst in Colorado's San Luis Valley, one of the most economically complex rural regions in the American West. My work is fundamentally about connection: identifying where regional organizations lack capacity and bridging them to the resources that can help.

Day to day, that means conducting structured outreach to Economic Development Organizations and Chambers of Commerce across a six-county district — listening for where their capacity is strained, processing what I hear into qualitative and quantitative gap analyses, and reporting findings to state and organizational stakeholders who can direct resources to fill those gaps. I act as a middleman between where things lack and where help exists.

Alongside that outreach work, I contribute to larger regional planning efforts, including action plan sections of the Comprehensive Economic Development Strategy (CEDS), retail data analysis, and authoring Enterprise Zone and EDA reports for the San Luis Valley. These projects let me apply the data skills I'm building toward real policy questions.

My academic background is unconventional in the best way: I competed as an NCAA Division II cross country and track athlete at Adams State University while earning a BS in Business Economics and an MBA in Finance, both Summa Cum Laude. Managing 20+ hours of training weekly alongside a 4.0 GPA taught me sustained performance under pressure.

My long-term goal is to work as an economist and data analyst, bringing rigorous quantitative methods to the economic policy questions that matter most: regional inequality, labor market dynamics, rural development, and climate economics. I'm building that toolkit in R and Excel now, with SQL, Stata, and SAS underway through DataCamp.

Academic Paper · Macroeconomics · Adams State University, 2023

The Looming Recession

An analysis of middle-class debt, income inequality, and structural economic risk. Drawing on Piketty & Saez income share data, household debt-to-income ratios, and wage growth figures, the paper argues that the preconditions for a debt-driven recession mirror those of 2008 — and proposes policy interventions including tax reform, union expansion, and antitrust enforcement.

03

Academic Paper · Climate Economics · Adams State University

The Relationship Between Economic Inequalities and Climate Change

A review and synthesis of Stern, Nordhaus, Goulder & Schein, and Boyce on the economics of climate policy. Evaluates carbon tax vs. cap-and-trade frameworks, critiques discount rate methodology in cost-benefit analysis, and introduces Boyce's argument that economic inequality is itself a structural cause of environmental degradation.
